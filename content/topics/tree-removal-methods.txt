@steps
eyebrow: Overview
title: Work down the list, not up it
subtitle: Each step down the list adds setup, adds time, and puts a person closer to the material. Start at the top and take the first method that fits.
step: Tip it over | Fewest steps. One good notch and a clean fall path.
step: Bucket / lift + crane | Fast picks, high capacity available, aerial tricks in play.
step: Climb + crane | Same reach, less capacity to spare. Slower, pick by pick.
step: Bucket / lift + rig | No crane. Rope the material down, forward it out.
step: Climb and rig | Everything by hand and rope. The hardest option.


@two-column
eyebrow: Method 01
title: Tip it over
subtitle: Cheapest and quickest when it fits — which is why it sits at the top of the list.
left-header: Everything below has to be true
left-icon: ●
left: **No obstructions** in the fall path
left: Not so far gone it **explodes on impact** and throws sticks across the street
left: Room for a truck or machine to get a **decent pulling angle**
left: Lean runs straight toward or straight away from the drop zone — **side lean disqualifies the tree**
left: Notch area is **hard green wood**, or dead but not soft and punky
left: **No large seam** through the notch area to cut into your holding wood
right-header: Good candidates
right-icon: ●
right: Front yard trees leaning toward the road
right: Backyard trees that will fit, pulled over with a machine
right: Parking lot and boulevard trees near a street they'll fit in — these suit a bucket truck too
right: **One hard rule:** never notch a tall tree on spikes to reach green wood (next slide)


@welcome
eyebrow: Safety · Read this one twice
title: Do not notch a tall tree while on spikes.
subtitle: Climbing the stem to find green wood for the notch puts you in the strike zone.
body: The tips can hit the ground first and **bounce the butt of the log straight back into the climber.**
body: Someone I know **broke his leg** doing exactly this. He was lucky it didn't hit him in the head, or he'd be dead — and he was extremely experienced by that point. He just got lazy.
body: **Experience is not the protection here. The geometry is the same for everybody.**


@card-grid
eyebrow: Method 02
title: Bucket or lift, plus a crane
subtitle: For any tree you can't comfortably tip over but a crane can still reach — right behind the house, large front-yard trees, wide sprawling silvers and oaks. Farthest pick under 80% of the chart.
card: Bucket Access | Ample room to work the tree from the lift.
card: Crane Footprint | Space to set up and lower picks into a clear drop zone.
card: Clear Overhead | Few aerial obstructions between tree and drop zone.
card: Firm and Flat | 3% slope or less. Measure across the outriggers, not with a phone level.
card: Radius Run | Capacity calculated and confirmed for the trunk logs.
card: Slingable Trunks | Sections small enough against capacity to sling correctly.


@two-column
eyebrow: Method 03
title: Climb, plus a crane
subtitle: Reachable by crane but can't be felled. Farthest pick tops out at 70% of the chart — versus 80% from a bucket — because the lift tricks disappear.
left-header: Why capacity has to be higher
left-icon: ●
left: **Lift tricks mostly disappear** — tip tying and standing up far limbs to regain capacity can't be done from a climbing line
left: Climbing and tying **many small picks** takes far longer than reaching them from a lift
left: Short on capacity? **Notch or peel the limb toward the crane** while the operator booms up, before the pick separates
left: The farthest pick needs the ball **directly above its center of gravity**
right-header: Good candidates
right-icon: ●
right: Reachable by crane but **cannot be felled**
right: Long meandering machine access, or delicate paved surfaces
right: **Many targets** below the tree
right: Much of the material hangs **over a structure**
right: Rigging would mean fence removal or a wet yard


@two-column
eyebrow: Method 04
title: Bucket or lift, and rig
subtitle: No crane. Rope the material down and forward it out. Good candidates: trunks under ~40", limited crane access, ample forwarding-machine access, under half the material over a structure.
left-header: What we need
left-icon: ●
left: Ample space to **set the bucket up**, with most or all of the tree reachable from it
left: **Few ground targets** — anything movable gets moved first, which speeds the whole job up
left: A **path out for the material** — the bigger the opening for the forwarding machine, the better
left: **Room to let material fall** — distance to the nearest breakable thing decides how much protection work it takes
right-header: "Permanent" targets that often move
right-icon: ●
right: Old clothesline poles
right: Phone lines — most homes no longer have a landline
right: Bird baths and bird feeders
right: Fence sections


@steps
eyebrow: Access · Every rigging job
title: Walk the yard before you rig it
subtitle: Anything you can move out from under the tree gets the tree down faster.
step: Find the bottleneck | Locate the tightest point, then picture driving a machine through it with a huge log in the grapple. If it won't fit, the plan changes.
step: Count the turns | Sharp turns slow everything down. A straighter path out is worth more than a shorter one.
step: Note what you can't mark up | Fresh grass, gardens, seal-coated driveways, paver areas — and how close the wires run.
step: Measure the drop room | Distance between the material coming down and the next thing that could break is the biggest factor in how hard the rigging gets.


@two-column
eyebrow: Method 05 · Last resort
title: Climb and rig
subtitle: The hardest of the five. Every method above this one exists so we spend less time here.
left-header: What makes it hard
left-icon: ●
left: Hardest when a large section **hangs over something that can't move** — a house, a fence, a grill
left: The question that decides the day: **how much has to come down by rope** before we can tip the rest over
left: The **more room to drop the trunk**, the easier the job gets
left: **Dead ash:** don't use this method unless the ground is **100% unobstructed**
right-header: Good candidates
right-icon: ●
right: Lift or bucket access limited or nonexistent
right: Tree is too far out for the crane
right: Generally **under 30" in diameter**, with few targets
right: Forwarding-machine access still matters — clearing material opens up better felling options


@three-rules
eyebrow: Climb and rig · Tie-in
title: Save the tallest section for last
subtitle: The higher the tie-in and rigging points, the more mechanical advantage you hold over the material below.
rule: Tie In High | Ideally the tallest section has the least obstructed path to the ground. Attach yourself to it and rig off it.
rule: Take It Out Last | Lower limbs come off first, worked from above. The tall section comes out **last**, so you keep a high tie-in and rigging point the whole removal.
rule: Expect It To Fight | If the tallest section is the part hanging over a structure, expect the rigging to be harder.


@two-column
eyebrow: Large Removals
title: Dealing with the knuckle
subtitle: The knuckle is the oversized butt log left at the end of a large removal. Sooner or later there's a big log on the ground and no crane capacity to pick it up — you need options to get it out of the yard.
left-header: The 40-inch problem
left-icon: ●
left: Past roughly **40" diameter** it gets hard to sling a log and keep the pick long enough that the sling doesn't slide off when you cut it free
left: Crane parked **within 25 ft** of the tree? Generally a non-issue
left: Plenty of crane capacity means a **longer pick**, which lets the slings sit lower on the log
right-header: Your options
right-icon: ●
right: Favorable lean? **Tip it over.**
right: Nothing at the base? **Slab off big sections** onto a brush pile so the yard doesn't take the hit
right: Get a **forwarding machine** into the yard to control the pull while felling
right: Over ~40", **tip the log into the yard**, cut it smaller, then sling and deadlift it off the ground with the crane


@steps
eyebrow: Putting It Together
title: Ask these in order
subtitle: The first yes wins. Stop there.
step: Can we safely tip it over?
step: Can a crane reach it, worked from a bucket or lift?
step: Can a crane reach it, worked by a climber?
step: Can we reach it from a bucket and rope it down?
step: Then we climb it and rig it.
