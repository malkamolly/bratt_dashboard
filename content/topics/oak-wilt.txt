@three-rules
eyebrow: What Is Oak Wilt?
title: Three things to know
subtitle: A fungus that infects the water-conducting tissue of oak trees.
rule: The Cause | A fungus, **Bretziella fagacearum**, that infects the **water-conducting tissue** (xylem) of oak trees and plugs it up.
rule: The Impact | It **kills large numbers of oaks every year** in Minnesota and is spreading toward the northern forest.
rule: The Good News | **Several proven management techniques exist** to prevent or control oak wilt on a property.


@section-divider
number: 02
title: Susceptibility
tagline: Not all oaks are equal — the red oak group dies fast, the white oak group dies slow.


@two-column
eyebrow: Susceptibility
title: Not all oaks are equal.
subtitle: The oak's group tells you how fast the story ends.
left-header: Red Oak Group
left-icon: ●
left: **As little as 4 weeks** from branch infection to tree death
left: Trees **do not survive**
left: Root-graft infection kills the tree shortly after leaf-out the following spring
left: Species: **Northern red oak, Northern pin oak, Black oak**
right-header: White Oak Group
right-icon: ●
right: **2 to 5 years** or longer after first symptoms
right: Disease progresses **very slowly**, advancing a little each year
right: Trees can persist for years
right: Species: **White oak, Bur oak, Swamp white oak, Chinkapin oak**


@section-divider
number: 03
title: Identification
tagline: The telltale leaf, and how the whole tree declines.


@welcome
eyebrow: Identification
title: The telltale leaf.
subtitle: The single most recognizable sign — in both oak groups.
body: **Brown edges, green centers.** Browning continues inward toward the center and base of the leaf. This is the one symptom to burn into memory.
body: **Wilting starts up high** — usually at the top or outer crown — then moves rapidly downward. **Wilted leaves drop fast** and collect on the ground around the tree.
body: **Easy to spot in summer:** diseased red oaks stand out because of their bright red-brown foliage against the healthy green around them.

@card-grid
eyebrow: Symptoms · Red Oak Group
title: Fast and fatal
subtitle: Northern red oak · Northern pin oak · Black oak
card: Leaves | Brown edges, green centers; browning spreads inward. Wilted leaves drop quickly.
card: Crown | Wilting starts at the top or outer crown and moves downward fast. Bright red-brown crowns visible in summer.
card: Speed of Decline | Branch infection: full wilt and death in as little as **4 weeks**. Root graft: death after leaf-out the next spring.
card: Bark and Wood | **Bluish staining** on the wood under peeled bark. Cracking bark or fungal mats may appear on the trunk.


@section-divider
number: 04
title: Transmission
tagline: Two ways oak wilt travels — one starts it, one spreads it.


@two-column
eyebrow: Transmission
title: Two ways oak wilt travels.
subtitle: Insects start new centers; roots do most of the spreading.
left-header: Step 1 · Above Ground
left-icon: ●
left: **Insects start it**
left: Sap beetles carry fungal spores from a diseased tree to a **fresh wound** on a healthy tree
left: This is how **brand-new oak wilt centers begin**
right-header: Step 2 · Below Ground
right-icon: ●
right: **Roots spread it**
right: Nearby oaks naturally **graft their roots together**
right: Once one tree is infected, the fungus moves underground into its neighbors — **the cause of most new infections**


@steps
eyebrow: Spread by Insects
title: Sap beetles and fresh wounds
subtitle: How a new infection center gets started above ground.
step: Beetles find the fungus | **Colopterus truncatus** and **Carpophilus sayi** are Minnesota's two carriers, drawn to chemicals from oak wilt fungal mats between the bark and wood.
step: Beetles find fresh wounds | They are strongly attracted to the smell of **fresh, wood-penetrating wounds** — so a wounded oak visited by a contaminated beetle can become the next infection.
step: Spring wood is vulnerable | Mats form April–July on red oaks that wilted the year before. In that same window, oaks grow large **spring-wood vessels** the fungus infects easily.
step: One local exception | Oak bark beetles (*Pseudopityophthorus*) spread oak wilt in parts of the U.S., **but not here in Minnesota**.


@welcome
eyebrow: Spread Through Roots
title: The underground highway.
subtitle: Most new infections come from the fungus moving through grafted roots of neighboring trees.
body: In a Twin Cities urban study, the **majority of root-graft spread happened within 30 ft**; the farthest researchers still found infected trees was **about 50 ft**. More distance between diseased trees means less spread by root grafting.
body: **What makes grafting more likely:** the oak species involved (grafting is common among northern pin oaks), and tree size, soil type, and terrain — **sandy soil on flat ground is high risk**.
body: Grafts also form **between different oak species**, such as northern pin oak and bur oak — so mixed stands are not automatically safe.


@section-divider
number: 05
title: Prevention & Management
tagline: Four actions that work best when used together.


@card-grid
eyebrow: Prevention & Management
title: Four actions that work together
subtitle: Coordinated use of several actions is the best way to stop the spread.
card: 01 · Treat High-Value Trees | Apply **fungicides** to uninfected, high-value oaks you most want to protect.
card: 02 · Remove Wilted Red Oaks | Take out red oaks that continue to produce **fungal mats**, the beetles' spore source.
card: 03 · Dispose of Logs Properly | Handle and dispose of logs from wilted trees correctly so they **cannot spread spores**.
card: 04 · Diversify Your Planting | Plant a **mix of species** so a single disease cannot take an entire landscape.
